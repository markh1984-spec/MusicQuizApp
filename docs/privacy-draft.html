Privacy Policy

Last updated 3 September 2026.

Two different people read this page. If you played a quiz and have come here from a page of photographs, start at Players, teams and photographs — it is the section written for you, and it is first for that reason. If you run quiz nights using Quizporium, everything from Your account onwards is about you.

Who this is with

[your name], trading as Quizporium, is responsible for the personal data described below. Write to [support email] with any question or request about your data, including asking for a photograph to be taken down.

Quizporium is the software. The nights are run by quizmasters who subscribe to it — sometimes us, usually somebody else. What is collected in a room, and whether a night's photographs are put on a public page, is the quizmaster's decision; we hold it for them and do what they ask with it. If you know which quizmaster ran your night, they can take a photograph down themselves in seconds. If you would rather not ask them, ask us instead.

Players, teams and photographs

You do not have an account with us. There is no password, no profile, and nothing that ties a team name to your real name, your phone or your email address.

What a night collects. The team name you type in, which options you tapped and how quickly, your score, and a random token your phone keeps so the app still knows it is you if the wifi drops out. The token is meaningless outside that one game.

Photographs. If you send a photograph it goes on the projector in the room straight away — that is what it is for, and it is why there is no approval step in between. It is also kept afterwards, so the quizmaster has a record of the night: how many played, who won, what the room looked like.

The public gallery. A quizmaster can put a night's photographs on a page that anybody with the link can open. Two things are true about that page and both matter:

  • we ask search engines not to index it, and not to index the photographs on it. A page of a pub's faces should not be findable by searching somebody's name.
  • anybody holding the link can open it without signing in. A link that gets passed on has been passed on — there is no lock on it.

The quizmaster can switch any single photograph off, or take a whole night down again, in one press.

Getting a photograph taken down. Ask the quizmaster, or write to [support email] naming the pub and the date. It comes off the page and out of the app.

And one honest limit, because we would rather say it than be asked. Photographs are kept in a private, version-controlled backup. Deleting a file from that kind of store takes it out of the app, off the gallery and out of anywhere anybody can reach it — but it does not remove it from the backup's own history. If that matters to you, say so when you ask and we will clear the history for that night as well; it is a manual job rather than a button, so it takes a little longer than the deletion itself. We are not going to tell you a backup has forgotten something when it has not.

Team names in public. If a venue runs a league, the quizmaster can publish the table. That page carries team names and points and nothing else — no photographs, no scores per person, no venue contact details. Names are checked first, and one that might offend is shown as "Name hidden" rather than dropped, so nobody's position in the table changes.

Children. Quiz nights are often family nights. We do not build profiles of players, we do not advertise to anybody, and nothing collected on a night is used for anything beyond that night, its scoreboard and its photographs. If a photograph of a child is on a gallery page and you would rather it was not, ask — see above. We will not ask you to prove anything first.

Your account

This section is for quizmasters — the people who subscribe to Quizporium and run the nights.

Signing in. An email address and a password. The password is hashed with scrypt before it is stored, so nobody at Quizporium, us included, can read the password itself. Used to run your account and let you sign in.

What you create. Your quizzes, bingo games, venue records, advert slides, diary and settings. Used to run your nights and show them back to you. Your own packs are private to your account — we do not read them except with your explicit, time-limited support access, logged so you can see exactly what was done.

Your invoice book. If you use the invoicing side of the app, it holds your own business and bank details and your venues' business contact details. It is stored as ordinary data on our servers and is not encrypted at rest — we say that plainly rather than imply it cannot be read, and it is why the app asks for as little as it can get away with. No card numbers are stored anywhere in Quizporium, by you or by us. Invoicing is optional; a quizmaster who would rather bill through their own accounts package simply never fills the tab in.

Billing. Which tier you are on, your subscription status, and an opaque reference from our payment processor. Your card number never reaches us — the processor holds it.

Your rooms' photographs and players. The photographs sent during your nights, and the team names and scores from them, are held against your account so your record of the night is yours. You decide what is published.

Why we are allowed to hold it

Running your account and the nights you launch is necessary to provide the subscription you signed up for. Billing data is processed to perform that contract and to meet our own accounting obligations. Photographs and team names from a room are processed on the quizmaster's instructions so they can run and record their own night, which is the legitimate interest both they and the room turn up for — narrowed by the fact that a photograph can be removed on request by anybody in it.

Who else touches it

  • Render — hosting. The servers Quizporium runs on are in Frankfurt.
  • GitHub — private repositories, where photographs and backups are kept so a night survives a server restart. Private, never a public repository, and a US company operating under its own safeguards.
  • A payment processor — once subscriptions are live. See their own privacy policy for how they handle billing data.
  • Spotify — where a night plays music through a connected Spotify account. Nothing about a player, a team or a venue is sent to Spotify; it is asked for tracks and playlists and nothing else.
  • Anthropic, OpenAI and Google — used to write quiz packs and draw the picture round. No photographs, no player data and no venue records are ever sent to them.

Nobody else. We do not sell data. There are no advertising trackers on any Quizporium page and no analytics that follow anybody around the internet.

Cookies and what is kept in your browser

Signed in as a quizmaster, a cookie keeps you signed in, and your console preferences are kept in your browser rather than on our servers. Playing a quiz, your phone keeps its join token and your team name so a refresh does not lose your place. That is the lot, and none of it is advertising.

How long it is kept

Account data for as long as the account is active, plus a reasonable period afterwards for billing and legal records. A night's photographs, team names and scores for as long as the quizmaster keeps them — that is their record of their own work — or until somebody asks for a photograph to come down, whichever is sooner. Close your account and ask us to delete your data and we will, with the same backup-history caveat set out above.

Your rights

You can ask to see what we hold, to have it corrected, or to have it deleted, and a quizmaster can ask to export their own packs. Write to [support email] and we will act on it — and if something means a request cannot be met exactly as asked, we will tell you what and why, rather than say it has been done when it has not. If you are not happy with how we have handled it you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Changes to this policy

We may update this policy from time to time. The new version goes up here with a new date at the top.
