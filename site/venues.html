How the night runs

I turn up and set up

Screen or projector, sound, and the whole quiz on a laptop. You need a plug and somewhere to point it.

They scan a code and pick a team name

On the big screen and read out. No app, no sign-up, no email address — which means nobody is left out and nothing holds the room up.

Rounds of twenty-second questions

Music, general knowledge, pictures, intros. Answer quickly and score more, so the room stays loud rather than thoughtful.

Scores on the wall as you go

Nothing to mark, no arguments, no ten-minute gap while somebody adds up. The night keeps moving and people keep drinking.

Why it works for a venue

It fills a dead night

A regular quiz gives people a reason to pick your Wednesday over somebody else's. Teams book tables, and they come back next week.

They stay longer

No marking break means the night runs on. Rounds are short, the gaps are small, and nobody drifts off between them.

Your offers on the screen

Pizza deal, a drink you want to shift, next month's live act — up between rounds on the same screen the quiz is on.

Everybody can join in

Anybody with a phone plays, including somebody who turned up on their own. Teams can share one phone if they would rather.

Photos of your night

People send silly pictures to the big screen during the quiz. You get a room laughing at itself — and something to post on Monday.

Christmas and themed nights

Halloween, Valentine's, Christmas — same quiz, dressed for the night, with a pack written for the occasion.
