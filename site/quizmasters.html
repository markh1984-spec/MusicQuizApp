The software behind the night

Your quiz night.
Your name on the screen.

Built by a working quizmaster, for the way a night actually runs: in a dark pub, on a phone, with sixty people waiting and no time to work anything out. Everything you need to run it, and nothing you have to think about while you are on the mic.

Ask for a look What you get

Running your own quiz at your own pub? This is for you too. The questions are written for you and the marking does itself — what you bring is the microphone and the personality. If that sounds like more than you fancy and you are in Essex, Kent or Surrey, have me come and do it instead.

What you get

Quizzes written for you

Whole packs, ready to run. Every question is checked by a second pass before it reaches you, and read through by a human after that.

Five kinds of round

General knowledge, whose face is this, name that intro, pick-them-all, and first letter only — where spelling does not count.

Music bingo

A completely different night from the same kit. Cards on their phones, you play a chorus and move on.

A control view for your phone

One big button that always does the next thing. The answers, the fastest finger and who picked what — on your screen, never on theirs.

Your colours and your name

The projector says your quiz, not somebody else's brand. Pick your two colours and every screen in the room follows.

Write your own as well

Your own quizzes and bingo games, in your own library, kept private — including from me.

Photos on the big screen

The room sends pictures of itself with dog ears on. It is the bit people talk about afterwards.

Past gigs, kept

Every night you have run, with the numbers and the photos — which is what a venue asks to see before they book you.

Invoicing

Bill the venue before you have left the car park, with your details and theirs kept from last time.

Built for a Wednesday going wrong

It comes back

A flat laptop, a dropped connection, a restart mid-round — the night resumes on the same question with every score intact, and phones put themselves back on their own.

Pub wifi is assumed, not hoped for

Built for bad connections and venue routers rather than a demo on a fast desk. Three hundred phones is a number that has been measured, not guessed.

A mis-tap cannot reveal an answer

Press the same button twice by accident and it happens once. The reveal refuses to fire in the first few seconds, because nobody has answered yet.

Nothing to install, for anybody

No app for the room, no app for you. A browser on whatever you have got, and a code on the screen.
